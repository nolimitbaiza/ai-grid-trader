01

Buy low, sell high

The agent charges in the overnight lull and the midday solar dip, then sells into the morning and evening peaks.

02

Every cycle has a price

Each MWh through the battery carries a wear cost. Trades that don't beat it are skipped, so the asset lasts longer.

03

Plans on forecasts, not hindsight

It trades on an imperfect forecast and re-plans every hour, the way a real operator must, and is scored on what actually happened.

How the agent decides

Every 15 minutes the agent picks how much to charge, discharge or hold. Its reward is:

reward = arbitrage cash − battery wear − micro-cycling penalty

The state is the time, the battery's charge level and whether it is currently charging, discharging or holding. The agent's decision table is computed with exact Bellman updates, which give the same answer a fully trained Q-learning agent converges to.

In Forecast mode the agent never sees the real future. Every hour it receives a fresh price forecast (the actual prices plus simulated forecast error that grows with lead time and smooths out peaks and dips), re-plans the rest of the horizon, and executes the next hour. Perfect hindsight plans on the actual prices and shows the theoretical maximum.

For the real markets, the full-year results run the same agent through all 8,760 hours of 2025 (re-planning every hour on a 24-hour forecast), so annual revenue and seasonality come from real prices rather than from scaling up two days.
